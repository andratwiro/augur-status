Getting help

One person maintains Augur. The numbers below are the ones that person can actually hit, not the ones that would look better on a pricing page. If a promise here is ever missed, it is a bug in the promise.

Where to write

hi@augur.works Anything at all — questions, bugs, billing, "my publish is stuck".
abuse@augur.works Report content hosted on Augur that shouldn't be. Read by a human, not a filter.
legal@augur.works Legal notices, data-protection requests, takedowns.

All three land in the same inbox. There is no ticket portal, no chat widget and no phone number.

How long you will wait

Free and open source

No response time. Best effort.

Mail sent from a free workspace is read, and often answered the same week — but nothing is promised, and some of it will never get a reply. That is the honest trade for a product that costs nothing.

Two routes are usually faster than the inbox: open an issue on the public repository, where other people can see it and answer it, or fork the thing. Augur is MIT licensed and self-hostable on your own Cloudflare account. Nobody can take it away from you.

Paid workspaces

First human reply within two business days.

Business days are Monday to Friday, Europe/Madrid, excluding Spanish public holidays. Most mail is answered much sooner; two days is the number that survives a bad week.

A reply means a person read it and told you what happens next. It does not mean the problem is fixed — some fixes take longer than two days, and you will be told which kind yours is.

Abuse and legal notices

Acknowledged within two business days, and acted on without undue delay after that. Clearly illegal content is removed as soon as it is seen, at any hour. Everything else gets a decision and a reason.

During an outage

The status page is the single place to look. It is hosted away from the machines that serve Augur, so it stays up when they do not.

  • It is updated within about an hour of the maintainer becoming aware of a problem, and every few hours while the problem lasts.
  • There is no 24/7 on-call. Nights and weekends in Europe/Madrid are covered by whether the phone alert wakes someone up, which it usually does, and sometimes does not.
  • Every incident gets a short written account afterwards on the status page: what broke, for how long, and what changed so it breaks less.

What is not offered

No SLA with credits attached. No guaranteed uptime percentage. No same-day phone support at any price. If you need those, Augur is the wrong tool and saying so now is cheaper for both of us than saying it during an incident.

Your data, if you leave or we do

augur export walks the published content out of the store into a plain directory on your own disk, using only a publish token — no Cloudflare account, no cooperation from anyone. Run it whenever you like. It is the reason the answer to "what if you get bored of this" is boring.
